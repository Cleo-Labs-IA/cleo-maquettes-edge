What actually happened on 17 September

The fire and burn recall covers infrared and hybrid sauna rooms and standalone infrared kits carrying the Finnleo, Tylö, Polar, Radiant Health or Helo badge, plus custom sauna rooms built with panels marked Saunalogic, Infralogic, Carbonflex or Tylö on the control panel. They were sold at Costco stores and Costco.com, and through pool and spa dealerships nationwide, from May 2013 through July 2026, for between $6,000 and $13,000. In the US, Sauna360 logged 67 reports of the infrared panels overheating, melting, charring or burning, with no injuries reported. In Canada, the company logged 5 reports of incidents as of 10 September 2026, also with no injuries. In both countries, the remedy is the same: consumers must stop using the sauna immediately and contact Sauna360 to schedule a free in-home repair from a professional installer.

The bench recall is a different product line and a different hazard. Tylö Halmstad 2, 3 and 4, and Tylö Kiruna Hybrid 2 and 3 sauna benches were sold from July 2024 through July 2026 for between $6,000 and $12,000. CPSC first recalled the benches on 23 October 2025 after seven reports of collapse, including one consumer with head and neck injuries, and Sauna360 offered a retrofit repair at the time. By 17 September 2026, that repair had not held: the company received 77 more reports of benches breaking, with six injuries, including a second report of head and neck injuries and one sprained ankle. The expanded recall now covers about 6,000 units and comes with a new remedy, a professional retrofit with additional hardware plus a revised manual stating the weight limit; Sauna360 is telling consumers to seek the new repair even if they already received the 2025 fix.

Three details behind the 17 September actions

01

One group, five brand names

Finnleo, Tylö, Polar, Radiant Health and Helo all trace back to Sauna360, the group formed when Tylö and Helo merged in 2017.

02

Two hazards, two markets

The fire and burn recall runs in parallel through CPSC in the US and Health Canada in Canada, each with its own unit count and consumer notice.

03

One repair, now replaced by a second

The bench retrofit issued in October 2025 did not stop further breakages; a revised repair and manual followed eleven months later.

2017

Sweden's Tylö and Finland's Helo merge into TyloHelo, later renamed Sauna360, bringing Finnleo, Polar, Radiant Health, Tylö and Helo under one group.

The numbers behind the two actions

One number is the combined size of the fire and burn recall across both countries. One is how many extra failure reports piled up after the bench fix that was supposed to have closed the earlier hazard. The third is how many months passed between that first fix and the admission that it had not worked.

48,338 units

the combined US (41,580) and Canadian (6,758) recall of infrared and hybrid saunas and kits announced on 17 September 2026 for fire and burn risk

77 more reports

The real subject: a recall record tracks who was notified, not whether the repair held

Most coverage of 17 September treated the fire recall and the bench recall as two unrelated stories about the same manufacturer, one about defective infrared panels, the other about breaking sauna benches. Read together, CPSC's own record shows something more specific: a repair that a regulator and a manufacturer jointly presented as resolving a hazard, in October 2025, did not survive contact with real use. The retrofit closed the case file, but not the underlying failure mode, and it took 77 more broken benches and six more injuries, tracked only through the company's own incident intake, before a second, different remedy was issued. Nothing in the recall record itself distinguishes a fix that held from a fix that only looked complete until the next incident report arrived. Layered on top of that is the fire and burn recall's own structure: the same hardware platform, sold continuously since 2013 under five brand names, triggers two separate procedural actions, one from CPSC covering roughly 41,580 US units, one from Health Canada covering roughly 6,758 Canadian units, each counted, published and remedied on its own track, with no single cross-border registry linking a specific unit sold in one country to its counterpart sold in the other.

Why it matters for brands

Three groups should read past the headline count of saunas. First, any brand selling one hardware platform under several storefront or regional brand names, as Sauna360 does with Finnleo, Tylö, Polar, Radiant Health and Helo, should treat the underlying component design as the compliance unit that carries risk across markets, not the brand name on the control panel: the same infrared panel defect produced two separately counted, separately published recalls, one under CPSC's US process and one under Health Canada's, with no shared unit identifier between them. Second, a repair is not a closed loop the day it is announced. Sauna360's October 2025 bench fix was represented, at the time, as resolving the hazard; it took eleven months and 77 further failure reports, tracked only through the company's own post-sale incident channel, for that to be shown false. Compliance and product-safety teams should treat post-remedy incident volume as an ongoing signal to monitor, not a metric that stops once a recall notice is published. Third, brands selling comparable sauna heating equipment or infrared cabins into the EU should not assume the fire hazard here is a US-specific story: EN IEC 60335-2-53, the EU-harmonised safety standard for sauna heating appliances and infrared cabins, imposes materially the same protection against overheating and short circuit that the 17 September US and Canadian recalls responded to. The enforcement path differs, EU compliance is typically self-declared through CE marking and checked after the fact by national market surveillance, rather than triggering an immediate recall demand, but the underlying engineering defect, and the cost of a remedy that does not hold, travels with the product regardless of which market's procedure applies.

Two ways to read 17 September

The narrow read

On 17 September 2026, Sauna360 recalled about 48,000 infrared saunas in the US and Canada for a short-circuit fire risk, and separately expanded a sauna-bench recall.

The structural read

The same manufacturer used the same day to disclose a brand-new hazard spanning two countries and five brand names, and to admit that the fix it gave consumers eleven months earlier for a different hazard did not hold, generating 77 more failure reports before it offered a second repair.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly did Sauna360 and regulators announce on 17 September 2026?
Two separate actions. CPSC and Health Canada, together with Sauna360, recalled about 41,580 US and 6,758 Canadian infrared and hybrid infrared saunas and infrared kits, sold under the Finnleo, Tylö, Polar, Radiant Health and Helo brand names, because the infrared panels can short circuit, overheat and cause fire or burns. Separately, CPSC and Sauna360 expanded an October 2025 recall of Tylö Halmstad and Kiruna Hybrid sauna benches to about 6,000 units, after 77 more reports of the benches breaking, including six injuries, and issued a new repair after the 2025 fix failed to stop the failures.
Why did the same company recall two different things on the same day?
The two recalls address unrelated defects on different product lines, infrared heating panels on one side and sauna bench supports on the other, and each moved through its own regulatory timeline before landing on the same publication date. The fire and burn recall is a new hazard finding. The bench recall is the second stage of a hazard first identified in October 2025, reopened because the original retrofit did not prevent further breakages.
Does the EU require the same fire protection on sauna heaters and infrared cabins?
Substantively, yes. EN IEC 60335-2-53, the EU's harmonised safety standard for sauna heating appliances and infrared cabins, imposes protection against overheating and short circuit comparable to the requirement behind the 17 September US and Canadian recalls. The enforcement path differs: EU compliance is typically self-declared through CE marking and checked afterward by national market surveillance authorities, rather than triggering an immediate recall demand from a national safety agency the way CPSC and Health Canada did here.
