A material standard rebuilt around where the fill came from

GB/T 17685 is the base national standard China uses to define and grade down and feather as a raw material: what counts as down versus feather, how it is washed and processed, and what quality a batch has to meet before it can be sold as filling. The 2026 revision, issued through Announcement No. 9 of 2026, is the first update since 2016 and took effect on 1 September 2026. Its main technical changes are in the definitions section: it adds or revises terms for pre-washed down and feather, fine-washed down and feather, virgin down and feather, reclaimed down and feather, recycled down and feather, waterfowl feather, dust and moisture content. Those are not cosmetic wording changes. Virgin, reclaimed and recycled are now separately defined categories, and the standard sets quality requirements for each one on a different track: virgin down and feather carries a set of mandatory test items plus a separate set of reference items, while recycled down and feather is assessed against its own distinct requirements.

The real subject: a classification a lab test cannot supply on its own

Most coverage of a standard like this reads it as a quality bar: pass the test, meet the grade. GB/T 17685-2026 is built differently. Before any test result means anything, a batch of fill has to be placed in the right category, virgin, reclaimed, recycled, and a finished product has to be checked for whether it is pure down and feather at all or a blend with other fibers. Only the first case falls inside this standard. A blend is out of scope entirely, which does not mean it is unregulated, but it does mean the fill inside it is graded, if at all, against a different rule that a compliance file has to identify correctly on its own. That upstream classification is supplier-declared, upstream of any single lab test, and it is exactly the kind of structured, sourced product data, what the fill is, where it sits in the standard's categories, which rule actually governs it, that a label or a certificate of analysis does not automatically carry. Two garments can both say '90% down' on the care label and sit on opposite sides of this standard's scope depending on what the other 10% is.

Why it matters for brands

The direct exposure sits with outerwear, bedding and sleeping-bag brands that source down or feather fill from Chinese suppliers, since GB/T 17685 is the reference material standard those suppliers grade against, and downstream product standards apply it by reference unless they set their own rules. For a brand's compliance file, the standard's revision means the origin category, virgin, reclaimed or recycled, now needs to be tracked as a distinct, verifiable data point per batch, not folded into a general 'down content' figure, because the mandatory and reference test items differ by category. For any product where the fill is blended with another fiber, whether by design or by cost substitution somewhere in the supply chain, the exposure is different: that product sits outside GB/T 17685-2026 altogether, so a supplier cannot point to this standard as evidence of compliance for it, and a brand relying on a generic 'meets GB/T 17685' claim on a blended-fill product is relying on a claim the standard does not actually cover.

Frequently asked questions

What is GB/T 17685-2026 and when did it take effect?
GB/T 17685-2026 is China's revised national standard for down and feather, issued by the Standardization Administration of China through Announcement No. 9 of 2026. It took effect on 1 September 2026, replacing the previous version, GB/T 17685-2016, and governs the production, inspection and sale of down and feather as a raw filling material.
What actually changed compared to the 2016 version?
The revision adds or updates definitions for pre-washed, fine-washed, virgin, reclaimed and recycled down and feather, along with waterfowl feather, dust and moisture content. It also splits quality requirements by fill origin: virgin down and feather now carries a set of mandatory test items plus a separate set of reference items, while recycled down and feather is assessed against its own distinct requirements.
Does GB/T 17685-2026 cover down jackets and bedding filled with a blend of down and other fibers?
No. The standard explicitly does not apply to products made of down and feather blended with other fibers. That means a finished product with blended fill sits outside GB/T 17685-2026's scope entirely, so a supplier or brand cannot cite this standard as evidence of compliance for it, even if the same product's care label lists a down percentage.
