A weather condition, not a product defect, triggers the ban

The General Product Safety Regulations 2005 (GPSR 2005) is the retained UK regulation that requires anyone who supplies a product to place only safe products on the market, and to withdraw or recall those that are not. It is a separate, older instrument from the EU's General Product Safety Regulation (2023/988), which the UK does not follow post-Brexit. On 14 August 2026, facing prolonged drought and heatwave conditions, OPSS issued statutory guidance under GPSR 2005 covering domestic single-use outdoor barbecues, defined as units with a fuel tray, grill grid and charcoal that are not intended for refill or reuse. The guidance states these products cannot currently be considered safe and instructs retailers and online sellers not to supply them, in store or online, while the guidance is in force. Failing to follow it is treated as a breach of GPSR 2005, enforceable by OPSS and by Local Authority Trading Standards, with sanctions that include criminal penalties. The guidance covers England, Scotland and Wales; Northern Ireland is not covered by GPSR 2005 in the same way and is outside the guidance's scope.

Three nuances that separate signal from noise

01

The product did not change, the environment did

No design flaw, recall or new standard triggered this. A disposable barbecue legally sellable on 13 August became unsellable on 14 August because of drought and heatwave conditions the guidance itself names as the reason.

02

No end date, only a review condition

The guidance sets no calendar date for withdrawal. OPSS has said it will keep the guidance under review and withdraw it, in consultation with other departments and agencies, once weather conditions change enough to significantly reduce the risk.

03

Three nations covered, one left out, one across a different border doing it voluntarily

England, Scotland and Wales are bound by statutory guidance with criminal sanctions attached. Northern Ireland is outside it. The separate state of Ireland only asked retailers to voluntarily pause sales in July 2026, with no legal requirement behind the request.

OPSS publishes statutory guidance under GPSR 2005 for England, Scotland and Wales: disposable barbecues must not be supplied in store or online while drought and heatwave conditions persist.

From 14 Aug 2026

Retailers, including major grocery and DIY chains, remove disposable barbecues from sale in Great Britain. OPSS and Local Authority Trading Standards hold enforcement powers, with criminal sanctions available for breaches.

No fixed date

The guidance remains in force until OPSS, in consultation with other departments and agencies, determines that weather conditions have changed enough to significantly reduce the risk.

The real subject: sellability now depends on a variable outside the product file

A compliance file for a disposable barbecue normally answers fixed questions: does the fuel tray meet the relevant flammability and stability standards, is the warning label present, is the product correctly classified for transport. Every one of those answers can still be yes on 19 August 2026, and the product can still be unsellable in Great Britain. GPSR 2005 lets OPSS treat foreseeable use, in this case, use during drought and heatwave conditions, as evidence a product is unsafe, independent of whether it meets its design standards. That is a different kind of compliance risk than a labelling gap or a missing certificate: it is a status that a brand's own product record cannot resolve, because the record that decides it, the weather and OPSS's review of it, sits outside the brand's control and outside a fixed calendar. A brand tracking this the way it tracks a labelling deadline, checking once and filing the answer, will miss the moment the guidance lifts, or the moment a second heatwave brings it back.

Why it matters for brands

For any outdoor cooking, camping or seasonal-retail brand selling into the UK, directly or through a distributor, three things follow from 14 August. First, the affected category is defined functionally, single-use, non-refillable barbecues with a fuel tray, grill grid and charcoal, not by brand or SKU name, so every product meeting that description is in scope in Great Britain regardless of how it is marketed. Second, the obligation falls on whoever supplies the product, meaning retailers and online marketplaces as much as the brand itself, and a brand that keeps shipping into UK sales channels without checking retailer-side compliance is exposed to the same GPSR 2005 enforcement its distributors face. Third, and most durable, brands need a monitoring process for this kind of condition-based restriction, not just a calendar of fixed deadlines: a product can move from compliant to non-compliant, and back, without a single line of its own specification changing, purely because a regulator's read of an external condition changed. That is a different compliance discipline than tracking a gazetted amendment or a labelling threshold, and it is one more markets are likely to reach for as extreme-weather events recur.

Two ways to read 14 August

The narrow read

The UK temporarily stopped disposable barbecue sales during a drought and heatwave, a sensible wildfire-prevention step.

The structural read

OPSS used a 21-year-old product safety regulation to make an entire, correctly manufactured product category unsellable in three of the UK's four nations, based on foreseeable use under external weather conditions, with no fixed date for reinstatement: a compliance status a brand's own product file cannot answer.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly did the UK ban on 14 August 2026?
The Office for Product Safety and Standards (OPSS) issued statutory guidance under the General Product Safety Regulations 2005 stating that domestic single-use outdoor barbecues, units with a fuel tray, grill grid and charcoal that are not intended for refill or reuse, cannot currently be considered safe because of drought and heatwave conditions. Retailers and online sellers in England, Scotland and Wales must not supply them, in store or online, while the guidance is in force.
Does the ban apply across the whole of the UK and Ireland?
No. The statutory guidance covers Great Britain only, England, Scotland and Wales, and is not part of Northern Ireland's regime. The separate state of Ireland took a different, non-binding route: in mid-July 2026, Irish retailers were asked to voluntarily pause disposable barbecue sales during similar weather warnings, with no statutory guidance or legal requirement behind that request.
When will brands be able to sell disposable barbecues in Great Britain again?
There is no fixed date. OPSS has said it will keep the guidance under review and withdraw it, in consultation with other departments and agencies, once weather conditions change enough to significantly reduce the risk. Brands and retailers need to monitor OPSS's guidance page directly rather than plan around a calendar deadline, since the guidance could lift or be reimposed as conditions change.
