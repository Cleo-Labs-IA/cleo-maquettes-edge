What actually changes, and on which date

The Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026, notified on 6 April 2026 by India's Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), brings more than 90 appliance categories, kitchen and food-preparation appliances, cleaning and home-comfort appliances, personal-care and wellness devices, and commercial electrical equipment, under IS 302 (Part 1):2024, aligned with IEC 60335-1:2020. It covers appliances rated up to 250V single-phase or 480V on other systems, including DC-supplied and battery-operated products, and requires the BIS Standard Mark under a Scheme-I licence.

Separately, the Cookware, Utensils and Cans for Foods and Beverages (Quality Control) Order, 2026, approved on 15 January 2026, revokes the 2025 order that preceded it and narrows its scope at the same time: the 2025 order covered stainless steel utensils, the 2026 order drops them and now covers only wrought aluminium utensils under IS 1660:2024 and aluminium beverage cans under IS 14407:2023. And the Hand Tools (Quality Control) Order, first notified 22 July 2025, was superseded on 4 December 2025 by a new notification covering the same five categories, pipe wrenches, open-jawed spanners, ring spanners, pliers and adjustable wrenches, but moving the enforcement date to 1 October 2026. None of the three orders references the other two. Each was drafted, amended and timed on its own track. They converge only on the calendar.

Three details behind the 1 October deadline

01

One date, three unrelated orders

1 October 2026 is the general-enterprise compliance date for electrical appliances (IS 302-1), aluminium cookware and cans, and five hand-tool categories, drafted on three independent tracks.

02

The deadline moves with the seller

1 October applies to general (large) enterprises only. Small enterprises have until 1 January 2027 and micro enterprises until 1 April 2027, under all three orders alike.

03

Material, not product name, decides scope

The 2026 cookware order dropped stainless steel utensils, which the 2025 version covered. Only wrought aluminium utensils and aluminium beverage cans remain in scope.

22 Jul 2025

The Hand Tools (Quality Control) Order, 2025 is first notified, covering pipe wrenches, open-jawed spanners, ring spanners, pliers and adjustable wrenches.

4 Dec 2025

A new notification supersedes the July 2025 hand tools order, keeping the same product scope but moving the general-enterprise enforcement date to 1 October 2026.

15 Jan 2026

The Cookware, Utensils and Cans for Foods and Beverages (Quality Control) Order, 2026 is approved, revoking the 2025 order and narrowing scope from stainless steel and aluminium to aluminium only.

6 Apr 2026

DPIIT notifies the Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026, covering more than 90 categories under IS 302 (Part 1):2024.

1 Oct 2026

All three orders reach mandatory BIS certification for general enterprises, the tier covering large and medium manufacturers and importers.

1 Jan 2027 / 1 Apr 2027

The same three standards become mandatory for small enterprises, then for micro enterprises, under all three orders.

The numbers behind the 1 October deadline

One number is how wide a single order reaches on its own. One is how many independent orders landed on the same date without coordinating. The third is how much later the same rule applies to the smallest sellers.

90+

appliance categories brought under mandatory BIS certification by the electrical-appliances order alone, from vacuum cleaners and mixer grinders to water heaters and air purifiers

3

separate BIS Quality Control Orders, covering entirely different product categories and drafted on independent tracks, that all reach their general-enterprise deadline on 1 October 2026

6 months

the gap between the general-enterprise deadline of 1 October 2026 and the micro-enterprise deadline of 1 April 2027, under all three orders

The real subject: a calendar date that answers two different questions

Most coverage of these three orders treats 1 October 2026 as a single fact: BIS certification is now required. That compresses two separate questions that a brand actually needs answered into one headline date. The first question is which standard applies to a given product at all, and that answer can change between the version of an order a brand read last year and the version in force today: the cookware order's 2025 text covered stainless steel utensils, the 2026 text that replaced it does not, so a product in scope twelve months ago may be out of scope now, while aluminium beverage cans are newly in. The second question is which enforcement date applies to the entity placing the product on the Indian market, and that depends on an enterprise-size classification, general, small or micro, that has nothing to do with the product itself. A brand that answers only the first question and assumes the October date applies to it by default can either chase a deadline it does not yet face, or miss one it does, depending on which side of both questions it actually sits on.

Why it matters for brands

Any brand selling electrical appliances, cookware or beverage cans, or hand tools into India, directly or through a local manufacturing or importing partner, now has two separate checks to run per SKU rather than one. First, classification: does the product's exact material, voltage range or sub-category still place it under the current version of the relevant order, not the version that applied last year. A cookware line manufactured to a stainless steel specification that was compliant under the 2025 order is simply outside the 2026 order's scope; an aluminium beverage can that was not previously covered now is. Second, the compliance date itself is not the brand's to assume: it is set by the enterprise-size classification of whichever entity, often a local manufacturing licensee or importer of record, actually places the product on the market, general enterprises from 1 October 2026, small enterprises from 1 January 2027, micro enterprises from 1 April 2027. The Electrical Appliances order also allows goods made or imported before its implementation date to keep selling for up to six months, provided the stock is declared to BIS and certification has been applied for, which gives brands with existing Indian inventory a narrow but real window to plan around rather than a cliff edge. Getting either check wrong, the product's classification or the seller's enterprise tier, means tracking the wrong deadline entirely.

Two ways to read the 1 October deadline

The narrow read

BIS certification for electrical appliances, aluminium cookware and cans, and hand tools becomes mandatory in India on 1 October 2026.

The structural read

That date applies only to general enterprises, under three orders whose scopes changed independently of each other and, in the cookware order's case, of its own 2025 version. Small enterprises have until 1 January 2027, micro enterprises until 1 April 2027, and a product's own material or sub-category can move it in or out of scope entirely. A brand's real deadline depends on its classification of both the product and the seller, not on the headline date alone.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly changed in India's BIS certification rules on 1 October 2026?
Three separate Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Quality Control Orders reached their general-enterprise compliance date: household and commercial electrical appliances (IS 302 Part 1:2024, more than 90 categories), aluminium cookware, utensils and beverage cans (IS 1660:2024 and IS 14407:2023), and five categories of hand tools. From that date, products in scope must carry the BIS Standard Mark under a valid licence when placed on the market by a general (large or medium) enterprise.
Does the 1 October 2026 deadline apply to every manufacturer and importer?
No. It applies only to general enterprises, the BIS classification covering large and medium manufacturers and importers. Small enterprises have until 1 January 2027 and micro enterprises until 1 April 2027 to meet the same three standards. A business should confirm its own enterprise-size classification before assuming the October date applies to it.
Is stainless steel cookware still covered by a BIS Quality Control Order in India?
Not under the Cookware, Utensils and Cans for Foods and Beverages (Quality Control) Order, 2026, approved on 15 January 2026, which revoked the 2025 order and narrowed scope to wrought aluminium utensils under IS 1660:2024 and aluminium beverage cans under IS 14407:2023 only. Brands should re-check their own cookware material against the current order rather than relying on the broader, stainless-steel-inclusive scope of the 2025 version.
