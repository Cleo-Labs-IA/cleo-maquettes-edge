On 30 July, the US recalled 121,680 Galanz retro refrigerators for a fire risk under five model numbers: Health Canada published its own recall 56 days later, naming only one

On 30 July 2026, CPSC and Galanz Americas recalled about 121,680 retro-style refrigerators in the US under five model numbers and four colours, after 34 fire reports including one linked to a death. Fifty-six days later, on 24 September 2026, Health Canada published its own recall notice for the identical defect and date-code window, naming only one of the five models, the red GLR76TRDER, after logging 2 Canadian incident reports with no injuries as of 21 September 2026. Neither notice explains the narrower Canadian scope, but it points to a recurring compliance-data problem: a recall's model list tracks what a market's own distribution records show was sold there, not the full reach of the underlying defect, and each market runs its own incident intake and publication timeline.

What actually happened, and 56 days apart

The US recall covers refrigerators sold in black, blue, red and white under model numbers BCD-215V-62H, GLR76TRDER, GLR76TBKER, GLR76TBEER and GLR76TWEER, each with a date code in YYYYMM format printed on a label on the back of the unit. Only units with date codes from December 2018 through December 2020 are included. They were sold at Home Depot stores nationwide and on Amazon.com from January 2019 through September 2022, for between $330 and $520. The 7.6 cu. ft. units have a top freezer with its own door, one drawer and a left- or right-hand opening main door. CPSC's count of 34 fire reports includes one the local fire department linked to a death; the recall's remedy is a free in-home repair, and consumers are told to stop using the unit and unplug it immediately.

Health Canada's notice, published 24 September 2026, describes the same short-circuit and ignition risk, the same 147 cm by 61 cm by 53 cm dimensions, and the same December 2018 to December 2020 date-code window. But where the US notice lists five model numbers across four colours, the Canadian notice names one: GLR76TRDER, red only. As of 21 September 2026, three days before publication, Galanz had received 2 incident reports in Canada, with no reports of injury. The remedy offered is the same free in-home repair as in the US, arranged through the same Galanz customer service line.

The real subject: a recall's model list tracks what a market received, not what the defect touches

Most coverage of the US recall treated it as the complete story: 121,680 retro refrigerators, five model numbers, one manufacturer. Read against Health Canada's notice, a narrower fact appears. The underlying electrical defect is not confined to one colour or one model number, the shared date-code window makes that clear, yet Canada's own recall names only the red GLR76TRDER. The simplest explanation is also the most useful one for a compliance team: Health Canada's notice describes what Galanz's Canadian distribution records show was actually sold there, not the full US model range. A hazard finding on a manufacturing platform does not automatically produce an identical recalled-SKU list in every market that platform reached; it produces whatever list each market's own import and retail records support. A consumer, or a compliance team, reading only the larger, earlier US notice would have no way to know that Canada's own recall, when it eventually arrived, would cover a single model rather than five, or that it would arrive 56 days later on its own regulatory timeline.

Why it matters for brands

Three groups should read past the headline unit count. First, any brand selling one manufacturing platform under several model numbers or colour variants across multiple markets needs a per-country record of exactly which SKUs were actually shipped where, because a hazard finding on the platform will not translate into an identical recalled-model list abroad; it will translate into whatever each market's own distribution data supports, as Health Canada's single-model notice shows against the US's five. Second, incident counts do not aggregate across borders automatically: CPSC's 34 US fire reports and Health Canada's 2 Canadian reports, as of 21 September 2026, come from two separate national intake channels tracking the same underlying defect, and a brand relying on a single global severity figure risks missing what a smaller market's own regulator is independently recording. Third, a first, larger recall in one market is not the end of the monitoring obligation. Fifty-six days passed between the US action and Canada's own notice for the identical hazard; compliance teams selling the same platform into several countries should expect, and plan resourcing for, a second or third national recall landing weeks or months after the first, each running on that market's own regulatory clock rather than a shared global one.

Two ways to read the 56-day gap

The narrow read

The US recalled 121,680 Galanz retro refrigerators on 30 July 2026 for a fire risk; Canada recalled the same defect on 24 September 2026.

The structural read

Fifty-six days after the US recall named five model numbers, Canada's own notice for the identical defect named one, the model its own distribution records show was actually sold there, a reminder that a recall's scope tracks each market's own records, not the underlying hazard.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly was recalled, and when?
On 30 July 2026, CPSC and Galanz Americas recalled about 121,680 retro-style refrigerators in the US, sold under model numbers BCD-215V-62H, GLR76TRDER, GLR76TBKER, GLR76TBEER and GLR76TWEER with date codes from December 2018 through December 2020, because internal electrical components can short circuit and cause fire and burn hazards. CPSC had logged 34 fire reports, including one linked to a death. On 24 September 2026, Health Canada published its own recall notice for the same defect and date-code window, naming only the red GLR76TRDER model, after Galanz logged 2 incident reports in Canada with no injuries as of 21 September 2026.
Why does Canada's recall cover only one model when the US recall covers five?
Health Canada's notice does not explain the difference, but the most consistent reading is that it reflects what Galanz's own Canadian distribution records show was actually sold in Canada, the red GLR76TRDER, rather than the full four-colour, five-model range sold in the US. The underlying electrical defect is not described as colour-specific in either notice; the date-code window is identical in both.
Does the 56-day gap mean Canadian consumers were at risk longer?
It means Canada's own recall notice, and the free-repair remedy it triggers, became publicly available 56 days after the US notice, not that the underlying hazard itself changed. Health Canada's notice reflects the timeline of Galanz's own incident reporting and distribution records in Canada, which is separate from, and did not automatically follow, the US recall's publication date.
