Thailand's two-year grace period for old food labels ends 19 July: stock still on a Thai shelf becomes illegal that day, no matter when it was made

On 19 July 2024, Thailand replaced its prepackaged-food labeling rule (Notification No. 367) with Notification No. 450, adding shellfish and squid to the mandatory allergen list and giving the market a two-year window to sell through old-format stock. That window closes 19 July 2026. Most coverage treats this as a routine grace-period expiry. What it misses: the clock runs from the notification's effective date, not from any product's manufacture date, so a batch labeled in 2023 becomes illegal to sell the moment it's still on a Thai shelf on 19 July 2026, regardless of when it left the factory.

What actually changed, and what the deadline actually gates

Notification No. 450, issued under Thailand's Food Act B.E. 2522 (1979), consolidates and replaces Notification No. 367 and its amendments as the country's core rule for labeling prepackaged food. It applies to any prepackaged food manufactured in Thailand or imported for sale there, which puts every foreign brand selling into the Thai market squarely in scope alongside domestic producers. Among the substantive changes: the mandatory allergen list gains shellfish (molluscs) and squid as declarable categories for the first time, Notification 367 only required crustacea (crab, shrimp, mantis shrimp, lobster) to be flagged, so a product containing clams, mussels, oysters, scallops or squid did not previously need an allergen statement for them and now does. The definition of "best before" is also realigned with Codex Alimentarius, tying the date to the point a food stops meeting its claimed quality rather than a looser shelf-life estimate. English-language equivalents are explicitly permitted alongside Thai text, which eases translation but does not reduce what has to be declared.

Three nuances that separate signal from noise

01

The clock runs from the notification, not from your production line

The two-year window is measured from 19 July 2024, the date Notification 450 took effect: not from the date any given batch was manufactured or imported.

02

It's a shelf test, not a factory-gate test

What matters on 19 July 2026 is whether a Notification-367-format label is still being offered for sale in Thailand that day, wherever it physically sits in the supply chain.

03

The allergen list added a category, not just extra fine print

Molluscs and squid were never on Thailand's mandatory allergen list before Notification 450 : only crustacea was. A crustacean-only allergen map built for Thailand is now incomplete.

The numbers behind the deadline

The transition is easy to summarize and easy to underestimate: two years, one new allergen category, one fine schedule that hasn't changed since 1979. None of those figures individually looks alarming. Together, they describe a market where the compliance bar just moved on ingredient data specifically: not packaging aesthetics, not country-of-origin marks, but the structured list of what has to be declared as an allergen and how a shelf-life claim has to be defined.

The real subject: a shelf-date rule, not a production-date rule

Framed as a routine grace period, 19 July 2026 sounds like a formality for anyone who updated their label artwork sometime in the last two years. Framed correctly, it is a test applied to physical inventory on a specific day. A batch produced and labeled in 2023 under Notification 367, sitting in a distributor's warehouse or a retailer's back room in Thailand, does not become compliant just because two years have passed since it was made: it becomes non-compliant the moment the calendar reaches 19 July 2026, regardless of when it left the factory. That is a meaningfully different operational problem than a forward-looking production cutoff: it means every SKU with slow inventory turnover, long import transit times, or stock sitting in bonded warehouses pending customs clearance needs a check against manufacture-and-label date, not just against whether current production runs have switched over. Brands that assumed "we updated our artwork" solved the problem may still be sitting on stranded, unsellable stock they haven't accounted for.

Why it matters for brands

This reaches any brand selling prepackaged food into Thailand, whether manufactured locally or imported: Notification 450 draws no distinction between the two. Two separate obligations converge on the same date. First, an inventory question: any SKU that hasn't turned over since mid-2024 needs its actual label, not just its current production template, checked against Notification 450, because the rule tracks what's on the label sitting on the shelf, not what a brand's design files say today. Second, a data question: a brand's allergen matrix for the Thai market now has to carry shellfish (molluscs) and squid as distinct declarable categories, which a template built around the EU's fourteen-allergen list or the US's major-allergen list does not automatically capture: those frameworks group molluscs and crustacea differently, and neither maps one-to-one onto Thailand's own list. A global allergen taxonomy that treats "shellfish" as one undifferentiated bucket, rather than a market-specific declarable category, is exactly the kind of gap this deadline exposes on 19 July.

Two ways to read 19 July

The narrow read

A two-year label transition period set in 2024 runs out on schedule. Routine housekeeping for any brand that already updated its packaging artwork.

The structural read

The rule tests the shelf on 19 July, not the factory floor, so stranded old-label stock anywhere in a Thai supply chain becomes illegal that day, and the allergen matrix behind every label now needs a Thailand-specific molluscs-and-squid category that most global templates don't carry.

Frequently asked questions

What happens in Thailand on 19 July 2026 for food labels?
The two-year transition period under Thailand's Ministry of Public Health Notification No. 450 B.E. 2567 (2024) ends. Notification 450, which took effect 19 July 2024, replaced the previous prepackaged-food labeling rule (Notification No. 367) and allowed food already labeled to the old standard to keep being sold for two years. From 19 July 2026, any prepackaged food manufactured in Thailand or imported for sale there must carry a Notification-450-compliant label; stock still bearing a Notification-367-format label can no longer legally be sold, regardless of when it was produced.
What allergens does Thailand's Notification 450 add to the mandatory labeling list?
Notification 450 adds shellfish (molluscs) and squid as mandatory declarable allergen categories. Under the previous Notification 367, only crustacea (crab, shrimp, mantis shrimp, lobster) had to be flagged, so products containing clams, mussels, oysters, scallops or squid did not previously require an allergen statement for them.
What is the penalty for selling food with a non-compliant label in Thailand?
Under Section 51 of Thailand's Food Act B.E. 2522 (1979), violating a labeling notification issued under Section 6(10), the provision Notification 450 falls under, carries a fine of up to 30,000 Thai baht.
