What actually changes on 6 August

The European Commission adopted Regulation (EU) 2023/1464 on 14 July 2023, amending Annex XVII of the REACH Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006 by adding entry 77, a new restriction on formaldehyde and formaldehyde-releasing substances in articles. The restriction sets two emission limits, measured by a chamber test defined in the regulation's Appendix 14, which references the reference conditions of EN 16516 (23±0.5°C, 45±3% relative humidity, a loading factor of 1±0.02 m²/m³, and an air exchange rate of 1±0.05 h⁻¹): a 0.062 mg/m³ ceiling for wood-based panels and furniture, and a 0.080 mg/m³ ceiling for all other in-scope articles. Both apply from the same date, 6 August 2026. A third, separate limit: also 0.062 mg/m³: applies specifically to the interior fittings of road vehicles, but not until 6 August 2027, a full year later. Three categories of article fall outside the restriction: those where formaldehyde is exclusively naturally present in the material the article is made from, those intended exclusively for outdoor use under reasonably foreseeable conditions, and those intended exclusively for industrial or professional use, unless that use still exposes the general public to the emissions.

Three nuances that separate signal from noise

01

"Furniture" is one of two tiers, not the whole rule

Wood-based panels and furniture face the stricter 0.062 mg/m³ ceiling. Every other article that releases formaldehyde into indoor air under normal use (toys, foam products, laminates, resin-bonded housings) faces the 0.080 mg/m³ ceiling, on the exact same 6 August 2026 date.

02

Vehicle interiors run on a separate clock

Interior fittings of road vehicles face the same 0.062 mg/m³ ceiling as furniture, but the date of application is 6 August 2027, one year later, for materials that can be chemically similar to what furniture makers must already comply with in 2026.

03

The exemptions are narrow, not a "we’re niche" out

Only naturally-occurring formaldehyde, exclusively-outdoor articles, and exclusively industrial/professional articles with no public exposure are excluded. Everything else that off-gasses indoors falls under one of the two limits, regardless of product category.

The numbers behind entry 77

One figure is the stricter ceiling that furniture and wood-based panels face. One is the looser, but still mandatory, ceiling that catches everything else. The third is the gap between the general deadline and the one carved out for vehicle interiors.

0.080 mg/m³

The real subject: a hazard-based restriction, not a product-category rule

REACH restrictions under Annex XVII don't work like a vertical product regulation that names a category and stops there. They attach to a substance and a use (here, formaldehyde released into indoor air) and then sweep in every 'article' that meets that description, regardless of what industry code it sits under. That is structurally different from reading entry 77 as 'the furniture rule': the operative question for any given SKU is not which product category it belongs to, but whether it is wood-based (0.062 mg/m³), falls into the broader 'other articles' bucket (0.080 mg/m³), qualifies for one of the three narrow exemptions, and, either way, whether there is Appendix 14 / EN 16516 chamber-test evidence on file to prove which side of the relevant threshold it sits on. That is a material-composition-and-test-data question, resolved SKU by SKU, not a checklist a brand can clear by confirming it doesn't sell sofas.

Why it matters for brands

This reaches far beyond furniture and homeware brands. Toy makers, appliance and electronics brands with wood or laminate housings, and any consumer-goods brand using foam, adhesives or resin-bonded components are directly in scope of the 0.080 mg/m³ tier, whether or not 'furniture' ever appears in their product taxonomy. The task before 6 August is not reformulation for most SKUs; it is classification: knowing, at the SKU level, which products are wood-based and face the stricter limit, which fall into the general 'other articles' bucket, which genuinely qualify for the outdoor-only, industrial-only or naturally-occurring exemptions, and which of those SKUs already have current chamber-test evidence on file versus which don't. Brands that manage material composition and test documentation as structured, queryable SKU data can run that classification as a single check across the whole catalogue. Brands that manage it as static spec sheets and PDFs have to open every product line by hand, including the ones that were never flagged as 'furniture' in the first place, with eleven days left before the general deadline lands.

Two ways to read 6 August

The narrow read

It's a furniture regulation: wood-based panel and furniture makers check their sofa and shelving catalogue against a 0.062 mg/m³ ceiling before August, and everyone else is unaffected.

The structural read

Entry 77 is a hazard-based restriction that reaches any indoor-use article emitting formaldehyde, at one of two limits, meaning toy, homeware and electronics brands with zero furniture SKUs can still be out of compliance on 6 August if they've never run the check, while vehicle-interior suppliers get an extra year on the identical limit for materially similar parts.

Frequently asked questions

What does Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1464 actually require from 6 August 2026?
Regulation (EU) 2023/1464, adopted 14 July 2023, adds entry 77 to Annex XVII of the REACH Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006, restricting formaldehyde and formaldehyde-releasing substances in articles. From 6 August 2026, wood-based panels and furniture must not exceed 0.062 mg/m³ in formaldehyde emissions, and every other in-scope article must not exceed 0.080 mg/m³, both measured by the chamber test method in Appendix 14 of the regulation, which references EN 16516 reference conditions.
Does the EU's new formaldehyde limit only apply to furniture?
No. Wood-based panels and furniture face the stricter 0.062 mg/m³ ceiling, but entry 77 also sets a 0.080 mg/m³ ceiling, from the same 6 August 2026 date, for every other article that can release formaldehyde into indoor air under normal use, including toys, foam-filled products, laminated goods and resin-bonded housings. Only three narrow categories are exempt: articles where formaldehyde is exclusively naturally present in the material, articles intended exclusively for outdoor use, and articles intended exclusively for industrial or professional use where the general public isn't exposed.
Does the 6 August 2026 deadline apply to vehicle interiors too?
No. The interior fittings of road vehicles are subject to the same 0.062 mg/m³ formaldehyde ceiling as wood-based panels and furniture, but their date of application is 6 August 2027: one year later than the general deadline that applies to furniture and every other in-scope article.
