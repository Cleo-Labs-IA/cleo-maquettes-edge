What global product compliance actually looks like

Every product sold across borders is regulated from the first sketch to well after it leaves the warehouse. Take a connected bike helmet: for that single SKU, more than a hundred regulations apply.

  • R&D: material and design rules, PFAS restrictions, REACH declarations, CE directives, battery rules
  • Manufacturing: certifications and testing, different from one country to the next
  • Labelling: language, warnings, pictograms, mandatory statements per market
  • Customs: HS codes, duty rates, sanctions lists, import licences

A multi-agent AI pipeline for product compliance

MARIA scans 19,000 regulatory authorities across 106 countries, indexing and structuring the legal corpus. Every result goes through human review by legal experts.

Why a multi-agent architecture matters

Product compliance is not one problem: it is dozens of specialised, overlapping ones. Textile regulation has nothing in common with electronics. Customs classification has nothing in common with labelling compliance. MARIA splits the work across specialised agents, each optimised for a precise regulatory domain, and orchestrates them into one coherent result.

Why 2026 is the inflection point

Two forces converge. European regulation is accelerating: the CSRD, the AI Act, the ESPR, extended producer responsibility rules and dozens of sector frameworks extend the compliance surface every year. And by 2030, every product sold in Europe will need a Digital Product Passport, a structured, machine-readable record of its composition, origin, environmental footprint and regulatory status.
