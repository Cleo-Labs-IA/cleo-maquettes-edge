What global product compliance actually looks like

Every product sold across borders is regulated from the first sketch to well after it leaves the warehouse. Take a connected bike helmet: for that single product, more than a hundred regulations apply.

  • R&D: material and design rules, PFAS restrictions, REACH declarations, CE directives, battery rules
  • Manufacturing: certifications and testing, different from one country to the next
  • Labelling: language, warnings, pictograms, mandatory statements per market
  • Customs: HS codes, duty rates, sanctions lists, import licences

A multi-agent AI pipeline for product compliance

Cleo tracks 19,000 regulatory authorities across 106 countries and structures the legislation they publish. A compliance expert reviews and validates every result.

Why a multi-agent architecture matters

Product compliance brings together dozens of specialised, overlapping problems. Textile and electronics regulations follow different logics, as do customs classification and labelling. Cleo splits the work across specialised agents, each optimised for a precise regulatory domain, and orchestrates them into one coherent result.

Why 2026 is the inflection point

Two forces converge. European regulation is accelerating: the CSRD, the AI Act, the ESPR, extended producer responsibility rules and dozens of sector frameworks extend the compliance surface every year. And the ESPR regulation introduces a digital product passport, product group by product group: a structured, machine-readable record of composition, origin, environmental footprint and regulatory status.
